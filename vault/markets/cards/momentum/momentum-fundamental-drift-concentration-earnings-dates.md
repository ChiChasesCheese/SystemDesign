Bernard and Thomas (1989/1990) showed that post-earnings-announcement drift
is not smooth over its roughly 60-trading-day horizon — a disproportionate
share of the abnormal return clusters around the company's {{c1::next
subsequent quarterly earnings announcements}}, especially the very next one.
This concentration is the key evidence for *why* PEAD counts as
underreaction rather than some unrelated price-momentum effect: the market
is still catching up on what the original surprise implies for {{c2::future
quarters}}' earnings, and it only fully updates its belief when the next
announcement confirms or contradicts that implication — the drift, in other
words, resolves in bursts at the dates new public information forces a
reassessment, not as a generic, calendar-smooth price trend.

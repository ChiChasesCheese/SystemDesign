Fung and Hsieh (2001) showed that a diversified trend-following program's
return stream resembles the payoff of a {{c1::lookback straddle}} — an
option-like structure that pays off large and positive whenever an asset
makes a big, sustained move in either direction, and pays little in a flat
or choppy market. Trend followers earn this shape mechanically: a sustained
market crash (2008, 2022) is itself a persistent downward {{c2::trend}}, so
systems that go short in a confirmed downtrend are positioned exactly right
for it, generating strong gains precisely when a global equity/bond
portfolio is losing money. This is why managed futures are said to offer
{{c3::"crisis alpha"}} — a positively skewed, convex payoff that shows up in
the same crisis periods that hurt most other risk premia (carry, equity
beta, credit), which is what makes trend following a genuine diversifier
rather than just another return stream that happens to be uncorrelated in
calm markets.

## zh
Fung 和 Hsieh(2001)证明,一个分散化的趋势跟踪组合的收益序列很像
{{c1::lookback straddle(回溯跨式期权)}}的payoff——这是一种期权式结构,
只要资产朝任一方向出现大幅、持续的move,它就会带来又大又正的回报,而在平
淡或震荡的市场里回报很小。趋势跟踪者机械地赚到这个形状:一次持续的市场崩
盘(2008 年、2022 年)本身就是一个持续向下的{{c2::趋势(trend)}},所以在
确认下跌趋势后做空的系统正好站对了位置,恰好在全球股/债组合亏钱的时候获得
可观收益。这就是为什么管理期货被称为提供{{c3::"危机 alpha(crisis
alpha)"}}——一种正偏、凸性的payoff,恰好出现在其他大多数风险溢价(carry、
股票 beta、信用)受伤最重的危机时期,这正是趋势跟踪能成为真正的分散化工
具,而不只是碰巧在平静市场里不相关的另一条收益曲线的原因。

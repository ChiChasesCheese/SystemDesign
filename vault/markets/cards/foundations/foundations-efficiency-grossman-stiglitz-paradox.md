## Q
If markets were perfectly informationally efficient — prices instantly and fully reflecting all available information — would it make sense for anyone to pay for research, data, or analysis? What does the Grossman-Stiglitz (1980) paradox conclude from this, and what does it imply markets must actually look like?

## A
**No — and that's the paradox: perfect informational efficiency is self-defeating.** If prices already fully reflected all available information, an investor who spent time and money gathering and analyzing new information could never profit from it, because the price would already be "correct." But if nobody has any incentive to gather costly information, nobody does — and then prices stop reflecting anything but the passively available information, which means they are no longer fully informationally efficient.

Grossman and Stiglitz's resolution is that markets settle into an equilibrium *degree* of inefficiency, not a corner solution of perfect efficiency: prices must be inefficient enough, at the margin, that the marginal informed trader is just compensated for their information-gathering costs by the returns available from trading on what they alone still know, and no more. This has two direct implications: (1) "the market is efficient" cannot literally mean zero exploitable information — some inefficiency has to exist to fund the very process (research, data purchase, analysis) that keeps prices as accurate as they are; and (2) the amount of that inefficiency should scale with the cost of the information and the capital available to exploit it — cheap, easily-processed data (e.g., adjusted daily closing prices on large-cap US stocks) should be almost fully arbitraged away, while expensive, hard-to-process information (point-in-time fundamentals, alternative data, illiquid or obscure securities) should retain more.

## Q zh
如果市场是完全信息有效的——价格瞬间、完整地反映所有可得信息——那么还有谁愿意花钱去做研究、买数据、做分析吗？Grossman 和 Stiglitz（1980）的悖论从这里得出了什么结论？它暗示真实的市场应该是什么样子？

## A zh
**不会有人愿意——这正是悖论所在：完全的信息有效性是自我否定的。** 如果价格已经完全反映了所有可得信息，那么一个花时间和金钱去搜集、分析新信息的投资者永远无法从中获利，因为价格早已经是"正确的"了。但如果没有人有动力去搜集有成本的信息，那就没有人会去做——于是价格就只能反映被动可得的信息，也就不再是完全信息有效的了。

Grossman 和 Stiglitz 给出的解法是：市场会稳定在一个均衡的**无效程度**上，而不是完全有效这个角点解：在边际上，价格必须"足够无效"，恰好让那个信息知情的边际交易者，靠着交易自己独有的信息所获得的收益，刚好补偿他搜集信息的成本，不多也不少。这带来两个直接推论：(1) "市场是有效的"并不能字面地理解为"完全没有可利用的信息"——必须存在一定程度的无效性，才能为维持价格准确性所需要的那整套过程（研究、买数据、分析）提供资金来源；(2) 这份无效性的大小，应当与信息的获取成本以及能够利用它的资本量成比例——廉价、易处理的数据（比如大盘股的复权日收盘价）应当已经被套利得所剩无几，而昂贵、难以处理的信息（point-in-time 基本面数据、另类数据、流动性差或冷门的证券）则应当保留更多可利用的空间。

Measured against US Treasury bills over roughly a century (1926–present, Ibbotson/CRSP data), the historical US equity risk premium — the average annual return of stocks over the risk-free rate — has been about {{c1::6-7% per year}}, and even measured against long-term government bonds instead of bills it has still run about {{c2::3-4% per year}}. That gap is large relative to what standard economic theory says should be a rational reward for the extra risk of holding stocks — the starting fact behind the "equity premium puzzle."

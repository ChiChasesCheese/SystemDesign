An asset's price is the present value of its future cash flows, discounted at a rate that itself equals {{c1::the risk-free rate plus a risk premium}} — so expected return is not a separate quantity to be explained on its own, it *is* the discount rate. This is why Cochrane frames modern asset pricing around one central organizing question: {{c2::"why do discount rates vary across time and across assets?"}} rather than around forecasting cash flows, because most of the observed variation in prices comes from changes in discount rates (expected returns), not from revisions to expected cash flows.

## zh
一项资产的价格是其未来现金流的现值，而用来贴现的那个利率本身就等于 {{c1::无风险利率加上一个风险溢价（risk premium）}}——所以预期收益并不是一个需要单独解释的量，它**就是**贴现率。这正是为什么 Cochrane 把现代资产定价的核心组织性问题概括为：{{c2::"贴现率为什么会随时间和资产不同而变化？"}}，而不是去预测现金流本身，因为价格中观察到的变动，绝大部分来自贴现率（预期收益）的变化，而不是对预期现金流的修正。
